# Site Selection

Good monitoring sites are not necessarily the most scenic or biodiverse locations. For this project, the most useful sites are the ones likely to show visible change over time.

## Useful Site Traits

Prioritize sites with at least one of these traits:

- obvious disturbance gradient
- bare ground, gravel, fill, mulch, scraped soil, or exposed substrate
- early colonizing vegetation
- invasive plant pressure
- recent or repeated mowing, pruning, herbicide, construction, flooding, or soil disturbance
- stable landmarks for repeatable photography
- manageable site size
- contrast between adjacent habitat types, such as asphalt edge, gravel strip, disturbed soil, woodland edge, and mowed field

## Lower-Priority Site Traits

Sites can still be useful, but may be harder to analyze if they are:

- visually uniform
- too shaded for consistent imagery
- hard to access consistently
- lacking stable landmarks
- unlikely to change over the monitoring period
